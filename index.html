Research

Dissertation
Architecture Neutral Vulnerability Identification in Binaries Using Natural Language Processing Techniques

Compiled binaries can be lifted to architecture-neutral intermediate representations (IRs) that sit between assembly and decompiled C. Prior work on learning from lifted IR has focused almost entirely on LLVM IR. My dissertation utilizes a fixed detection pipeline (IR-specific tokenizer and ModernBERT encoder, followed by a BiLSTM classifier) and varies only the representation and compiler optimization level. The goal is to measure how a representation's choice and level of abstraction affect what vulnerability-relevant information a model can learn.

Representations compared: LLVM IR (baseline), Ghidra P-Code, VEX, and Binary Ninja's Medium Intermediate Language (MLIL), evaluated on the Juliet C/C++ test suite for stack-based buffer overflows (CWE-121), compiled with and without common optimizations.

Research questions

  1. Without compiler optimizations, how does the choice of IR affect the performance of a fixed vulnerability-identification pipeline?
  2. Under common optimization settings (-O1, -O2, -O3), how does IR choice affect detection, and how does this differ from the unoptimized baseline?
  3. To what extent does optimization level moderate the relationship between IR choice and performance, and which IR is most consistent across optimization levels?

Status: Pipeline built and preliminary results obtained for LLVM IR and P-Code on unoptimized binaries; Binary Ninja and VEX experiments are in progress.
